Why most DIY purifiers fail

Computer fans can't push through a HEPA cartridge.

A computer fan is axial: lots of air, almost no pressure. That works for a Corsi-Rosenthal box with half a square metre of flat filter — but a published smoke-decay test of PC fans on a cylindrical HEPA cartridge measured about 16 m³/h. A Xiaomi-style cartridge needs 135–160 Pa at its working point; an axial PC fan tops out near 100 Pa.

Every commercial purifier solves this with a backward-curved centrifugal blower. OpenAir uses the same class of fan — EC-controlled, PWM speed from the ESP32, tachometer feedback — so the pressure curve actually matches the filter.

Feature parity

What the mid-range commercial units do — and what OpenAir adds

Auto mode, sleep/turbo, child lock, timers, filter-life tracking, laser PM sensor, app control — plus two safety features most commercial units don't have.

🌀

Auto mode that doesn't hunt

Smoothed PM2.5 sets the speed. Steps up fast, down slowly, configurable quiet ceiling, optional full stop when the air is clean.

🛰️

Laser particle sensor

PM1 / PM2.5 / PM10 on the value build. The reference build adds CO₂, VOC, NOx, temperature and humidity via the Bosch SEN66.

🏠

Home Assistant native

ESPHome firmware: sensors, switches, number, select, light and siren entities, plus a local web UI. No cloud, no vendor app. Every feature also works standalone on the touch pads.

⏳

Filter life, counted twice

Tracks the air volume actually processed and calendar age — whichever runs out first. Alert at 10%, reset with a 5-second hold.

🧲

Safety interlock

A hall sensor and magnet in the twist-off base stop the blower the moment the base comes off. No exposed spinning impeller.

📢

Stall detection + audible feedback

The blower's tachometer reports a fault instead of stalling silently. Piezo beeps on every touch.

👆

Touch through plastic

Five capacitive pads work through the printed shell: power, mode, speed, timer, lock. Child lock by holding the lock pad for 3 seconds.

💡

Air-quality light

Six-colour LED ring or light line, auto-dimmed by an ambient light sensor. Becomes a speed bar graph after each touch.

🚫

What we left out — on purpose

No ionisers, no plasma, no UV-C. They generate ozone and by-products. A real H13 HEPA cartridge does the particulate job better.

Technical specifications

The details

Xiaomi 2/2S/3/3H/Pro compatible cartridge, 200 × 293 mm. H13-class pleated HEPA (~2 m² of media) + granular activated carbon layer. A$18–30 from AliExpress or eBay 4-packs. Generic cartridges vary in true filtration class — batch results will be published.
Value builds: 160 mm 24 V EC backward-curved plug fan, PWM in + tach out (~A$45). Reference: ebm-papst R1G190-AB27-02, Ø190 impeller, 24 V 51 W, public datasheet curve ≈495 m³/h @ 127 Pa. Operating point against the modelled system curve: ~420–460 m³/h at 135–160 Pa.
ESP32-S3 DevKitC-1 (N16R8). PMS5003 laser PM sensor (value) or Bosch SEN66 (reference). WS2812B LED ring or light line. 1.28" round TFT on the reference build. Hall sensor + magnet interlock, piezo buzzer, ambient light sensor, five capacitive touch pads. All low-voltage DC — no mains wiring inside the enclosure.
Value builds: USB-C PD trigger board at 20 V from a charger you already own (≥45 W recommended; 65 W for headroom). Reference: 24 V 3 A (72 W) brick. Standby well under 1 W.
PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle, no enclosure needed. 1.55–1.94 kg depending on build. Fits a 256 × 256 mm bed. Heat-set M3 inserts recommended for repeated assembly; self-tapping screws work for the first build.
ESPHome 2026.9. Home Assistant entities: PM1/2.5/10 sensors, CO₂/VOC/NOx (reference), fan speed number, mode select, filter-life sensors, child-lock switch, timer, light (LED ring), siren (fault alerts). Local web UI included. OTA updates.
Side Table 250 × 250 × 443 mm · Towers Ø240 × 467–507 mm. Noise estimates: ~25 dB(A) at sleep, ~50 dB(A) at maximum — to be measured with a Class-2 meter at 1 m and 3 m before shipping.
